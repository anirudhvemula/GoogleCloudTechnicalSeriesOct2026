**Summary of Persephone Analysis Report**

The Persephone_Analysis_Report.pdf summarizes five years of observations of the exoplanet Persephone gathered by the Stargazer and Odyssey space telescopes. The findings position Persephone as one of the most promising potentially habitable exoplanets discovered to date.

1- Host Star System (Xylos)
- Location: ~68 light-years from Earth in the constellation Cetus.
- Star Properties: Orbits Xylos, a stable, middle-aged G3V star (~5.5 billion years old) with 0.96 solar masses and 0.92 solar luminosity.
- Stability: Features low flare activity and coronal mass ejections, providing a stable orbital and radiation environment.

2- Atmospheric Composition
Spectroscopic data reveals an Earth-like atmospheric mixture:
Nitrogen (N<sub>2</sub> ): ~70% (dominant component).
Oxygen (O<sub>2</sub> ): 22–23%.
Water Vapor (H<sub>2</sub>O): High concentration, indicating vast bodies of liquid surface water.
Trace Gases: Argon (Ar), Ozone (O<sub>3</sub>), and Methane (CH<sub>4</sub>). The coexistence of oxygen and methane hints at potential biological or geological activity.

3. Physical Dimensions & Properties
- Radius: 0.97 R<sub>⊕</sub> (97% of Earth's size).
- Mass: 0.92 M<sub>⊕</sub>  (92% of Earth's mass).
- Density: 5.45 g/cm³ (very close to Earth's 5.51 g/cm³), indicating a rocky composition with a similar mantle/core structure.
- Surface Gravity: 0.97 G.

4. Surface & Geological Features
- Water & Ice: Substantial surface water basins and large polar icecaps, along with ice at high mountain elevations.
- Continents: Four widely distributed continental landmasses with indications of tectonic activity.
- Vegetation-Like Reflectance: Regions reflecting red and near-infrared light suggest widespread photosynthetically active land cover.
<br>
5. Climate & Habitability
- Orbit: Habitable zone orbit of approximately 412 Earth days with minimal seasonal extremes.
- Temperature: Mean surface temperature of ~18°C (64°F) with mild weather systems and storm patterns.
<br>
6. Biosignatures & Future Research
- Chemical Disequilibrium: Ongoing analysis of simultaneous oxygen and methane signatures.
- Biogenic Gases: Searching for trace biosignature indicators such as dimethyl sulfide.

Conclusion: Persephone represents a prime candidate for astrobiological study and future interstellar exploration initiatives.
